Space Datacenter Feasibility, Part 2

The Best Case, As Drawn and Packed

One satellite at 550 km in a dawn-dusk orbit, Starship at $100/kg, solar arrays at $10/W, a radiator designed down to the engineering drawing, and a bus built up in SolidWorks. Launch is priced the way Starship sells it: by the flight, so how many satellites fit in the bay matters as much as their weight.

Everything is per kW-yr of compute: the space premium (arrays, radiators, bus, launch; not the GPUs, which you buy either way) spread over the satellite's life. It wins if it beats what the ground costs. Read the blog post or the notebook.

What this models

  • Orbit: fixed at 550 km dawn-dusk sun-synchronous. Compute pauses in Earth's shadow and throttles in off-angle sunlight: 92.8% availability, year-averaged.
  • Array: sized for end-of-life power after the degradation you set. Its stowed volume (kW/m³) sets the folded-wing thickness, which can set the layer height in the stack.
  • Radiator: two vertical fins radiating from both faces, sized on the sunniest day after Earth IR, albedo and grazing sunlight. Fin efficiency is computed from the sheet thickness and tube spacing (80% for the drawn panel). More area means more panels in each stowed layer.
  • Bus: 596 kg bottom-up from the SolidWorks flat-pack (structure, wheels, lasers, thrusters, power conditioning, 8% unmodeled), plus a $500k fixed cost. Drag propellant at solar maximum is included.
  • Launch: flight price = $/kg × payload. Satellites per flight is the smaller of what fits by mass and what fits in Starship's tapered 22 m bay. The tower concept is fixed at its CAD result of 11.
  • Left out: batteries, structural vibration, deployment loads, Starship's reduced payload to a polar orbit, and GPU radiation life.
