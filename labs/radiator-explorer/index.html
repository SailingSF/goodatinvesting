GPUs in Space, Part 2

Where the Heat Goes

In space there's no air to blow over a heat sink. Every watt the GPUs burn has to leave as infrared light from a radiator, and a surface radiates as its temperature to the fourth power. Follow 132 kW from the GPU die to deep space, then see why the sheet between the coolant tubes runs cold and costs you area.

Inputs

Worst day of the year at 550 km: Earth's infrared, sunlight reflected off Earth, and sunlight grazing the fins add W/m² that has to be radiated away as well.

A slice through the 25 mm panel, three tube spacings wide. Coolant tubes are bonded to both aluminum face sheets; heat spreads sideways through the thin sheet and radiates off its outer face. Each dot is light leaving the surface, and hotter metal sends far more of it (T⁴).
